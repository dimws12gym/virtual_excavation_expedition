Expedition field guide

Your mission, step by step.

  1. Excavate. Click or tap the 24 grid squares to brush away sediment. Recover all 12 bone groups. Coordinates are added to the field record.
  2. Assemble. Select a bone card, then click its matching translucent outline. You can also drag a card onto an outline. Left and right refer to the individual’s own sides. Recovered bones become solid; missing groups remain translucent.
  3. Investigate. Compare the recovered pelvis with two reference patterns. Use both pelvic observations to choose the more likely biological sex.
  4. Measure. Read the maximum femur length from the osteometric board in centimetres. Enter it, then calculate stature using the selected equation.
  5. Report. Record your conclusion and save or print your field report. The completed skeleton stays visible in its burial.

No timer, no lives lost. Work at your own pace. All actions also support keyboard navigation: Tab to a square, card or outline; Enter or Space to activate.
Teacher’s method note

Evidence, equations & limitations.

These are fictional adult cases. Skeletons and pelvic outlines are simplified teaching diagrams, not diagnostic anatomical models. The case’s recorded pelvic traits provide the evidence for the exercise. Biological sex estimation does not establish gender identity.

Male: H = 2.38 × F + 61.41 ± 3.27 cm
Female: H = 2.47 × F + 54.10 ± 3.72 cm

F is maximum femur length in centimetres. H is estimated living stature. The equations requested for this activity are historically labelled “White” reference equations in the Trotter–Gleser tradition. This is a historical sample category, not a biological race determination. Ancestry is not inferred in this game, and these equations are not universal.

± is the standard error of estimate (SEE). It is not a guaranteed range, a 95% interval, or a measurement error. The report provides a central estimate and SEE separately. No age correction is applied in these fictional adult exercises.
